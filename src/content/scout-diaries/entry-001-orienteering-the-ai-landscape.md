> *A scout's job isn't to know every trail by heart — it's to read the terrain well enough to move safely through it.*

## Setting out

The AI landscape in 2026 looks nothing like it did eighteen months ago. New model families, new reasoning architectures, new tool-use patterns — the map updates before the ink dries. The classic response is either panic ("I'll never keep up!") or avoidance ("I'll wait until things settle"). Both are traps.

A scout takes a third path: **stay curious, travel light, and log what you find**.

## What a scout actually does

| Skill | What it looks like in AI |
|---|---|
| Reads terrain features | Understands *why* a model behaves a certain way, not just *that* it does |
| Carries minimal kit | Uses the simplest tool that gets the job done |
| Notes landmarks | Keeps a diary — hence these entries |
| Reports back | Shares findings so the whole team moves smarter |

## The diary format

Each entry in these *Scout Diaries* will follow a loose structure:

1. **Territory** — the slice of the AI world explored this time.
2. **Observations** — what was found, tried, or broken.
3. **Bearing** — a single practical takeaway for the next leg of the journey.

## Bearing for this entry

*You don't need to understand everything — you need a reliable process for understanding new things quickly.*

Set up your own scouting loop: read one new thing, try it in a small experiment, write one sentence about what you learned. That's the whole system.
